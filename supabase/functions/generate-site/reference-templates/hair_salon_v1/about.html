Started at a
kitchen sink,
in 2016.

Ten years later, the sink is a proper basin — but the standard hasn't moved.

"I trained in London and Copenhagen, and kept noticing the same thing: the best colour work happened slowly, in salons that turned people away rather than rush them."

— Freja Lindqvist, Founder & Colour Director

Noir & Ash opened in 2016 with two chairs and a waiting list within three months. The premise was simple: fewer appointments per day, more time in each one, and colour formulated by hand rather than pulled off a shelf.

That premise hasn't changed. We're still a small team — by design. Every stylist here trains for a minimum of two years alongside a senior colourist before taking their own bookings, which is longer than most salons allow and shorter than we'd like.

Today the studio sits on Södra Förstadsgatan, five chairs, one colour bar, and a client list that's grown almost entirely by referral.

What guides the work

Consultation before scissors

No cut or colour begins without a real conversation about texture, upkeep, and what "low maintenance" actually means to you.

Formulated, not shelved

Every colour is mixed to order against your skin tone and history — never a box formula run twice the same way.

Built to grow out well

A good cut or colour should still look intentional at week eight, not just on the day you leave the chair.

The studio

Five chairs, one colour bar, and a lot of natural light.

The studio was built around the colour bar — an open counter where every formula is mixed in view of the client, not behind a closed door. Large west-facing windows mean colour is checked and matched in daylight, never under salon fluorescents alone.
